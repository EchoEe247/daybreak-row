## Suggested phase structure (revise based on evidence)
**Phase 0 — establish reliable baselines:** physical Pixel 6a screenshots, FPS/frame-time/memory/download metrics, representative downtown and interior A/B scenes, controls, save-preservation tests.

**Phase 1 — focused realism pilots:** demonstrate the top 2–3 visually meaningful asset/material upgrades without exceeding proposed budgets.

**Phase 2 — world consistency and resident life:** consider coherent architecture/interiors, animation continuity, NPC behavior and stronger play loops, subject to prioritization.

**Phase 3 — deeper game systems and content:** user-approved feature expansion, sustainable world content pipeline, testing and performance work.

**Phase 4 — polish and distribution options:** onboarding, UI, audio, portability, QA and feasible delivery paths, after choosing product vision.

These phases are **scaffolding only**, not approved scope.

## For each milestone, document
- Why it matters to the player; current limitation and exact affected files.
- Dependencies and feasibility of the source assets, including verified licensing.
- Estimated effort (range, confidence) and any specialized skills/tools required.
- Technical approach and smallest useful first implementation.
- Risks, performance costs, fallback/rollback, backward-compatible saves.
- A measurable acceptance test on the **actual Pixel 6a**, including graphics and controls when relevant.
- Clear **must-have**, **should-have**, **later**, **avoid** categories.

## Decision gates
Separate the **research/choice** gate from the **implementation** gate. State which activities need user sign-off: product direction, external data/API usage, license-risk acceptance, asset additions, large dependencies, new internet requirements and any change to save data/daylight.
